Water quality

Hard water in San Antonio: scale, spots and tired appliances

Water in the San Antonio area is commonly described as hard, meaning it carries dissolved calcium and magnesium. Over time those minerals can leave scale behind. Here is what homeowners usually notice:

  • Chalky white buildup on faucets, showerheads and glass
  • Spotty dishes and glassware after the dishwasher
  • A water heater that rumbles, pops or heats slowly
  • Weaker flow as aerators and fixtures clog with mineral deposits
  • Soap that never seems to lather properly

Hardness varies by neighborhood and water source. Ask about testing your own water before choosing a system.

Quick answers

Plumbing questions San Antonio homeowners ask

How do I get a plumber in San Antonio?

Call Anchor Plumbing Pro at (210) 944-4234 or send a request on the contact page. Describe the problem, where it is and how long it has been happening. You will get next steps and scheduling options for your address.

What should I do during a plumbing emergency?

Shut off the main water valve if it is safe to reach. Keep people away from water near electrical outlets or panels. If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or your gas utility. Then call a plumber and describe what is happening.

Who fixes a leak: the San Antonio Water System or a plumber?

SAWS handles the public water and sewer system. Leaks on private property are generally the owner's responsibility and need a plumber. To report a public main leak or outage, call SAWS at 210-704-SAWS (7297) and press 1. If you are unsure which side a leak is on, ask SAWS or a plumber.

Do you offer emergency plumbing in San Antonio?

For burst pipes, main line failures and sewage backups, call (210) 944-4234 right away and describe the situation. Availability for urgent requests is confirmed on the phone for your address. The emergency plumbing page lists the problems covered.

Tank or tankless water heater: which is better?

A tank heater costs less up front and is simpler to replace. A tankless unit heats water on demand and saves space, but costs more to install and needs correct gas, venting or electrical sizing. In hard water, tankless units also need periodic descaling. The right pick depends on household size and your home's setup.

Do I need a water softener in San Antonio?

Water here is commonly hard. If you see white scale on fixtures, spotted dishes or a noisy water heater, a softener may help. Reverse osmosis improves drinking water at one tap, and whole-house filtration targets taste and odor. Start by deciding which problem you want to fix.

How can I tell if I have a slab leak?

Common signs are an unexplained jump in your water bill, the sound of running water when everything is off, warm spots on the floor, damp flooring and low pressure. To check, turn off all water-using appliances and see whether the meter's leak indicator keeps moving. Then call for leak detection.

How much does a plumber cost in San Antonio?

Cost depends on the problem, access, parts and whether it is a repair or a replacement. Ask for a written estimate that explains the options before work begins. Be cautious of any price quoted without a description of the problem.

How do I check that a plumber is licensed in Texas?

Plumbing in Texas is regulated by the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners. Ask the plumber for a license number and look it up with the Board before you agree to the work.
